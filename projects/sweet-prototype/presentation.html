Sensor Deployment Flow

Redesigning how security teams deploy sensors — and trust that their environment is actually protected.

Prototype path AWS → Kubernetes → Helm Deployment overview + guided install flow

The starting point

Deployment is the first thing users do — and the easiest place to lose them.

The problem

Before a platform detects a single threat, someone has to deploy sensors across their infrastructure. When that first step is confusing, customers stall before they ever reach the value they came for.

Who I designed for

A SOC analyst or DevOps engineer — technical enough to run a deployment, but not an expert in this specific product. They move between many security tools and lean on familiar patterns.

What I assumed

Design starts with a point of view. Here's mine.

  • 1
    Users don't know this UI — it has to guide them, not test them.
  • 2
    They trust familiar, industry-standard patterns over anything clever.
  • 3
    They need confidence that everything installed correctly.
  • 4
    They want to understand exactly what protection they're getting.
  • 5
    When something breaks, they need immediate clarity on what happened and how to fix it.

03 · Key decision · the big one

One "status" was hiding three different problems.

A generic status forces users to interpret. I split it into three independent dimensions, so anyone can tell instantly what kind of problem they're facing.

Health

  • Healthy
  • Warning
  • Critical
  • Offline

Connection

  • Connected
  • Connecting
  • Disconnected
  • Never connected

Deployment

  • Deploying
  • Updating
  • Failed
  • Installed

03 · Key decision

Every problem should explain itself.

Before

"Sensor upgrade required."

After

This sensor is running v4.7.0, no longer the latest supported release. Until it's upgraded, it may not report full telemetry or receive the latest protections.

Recommended: upgrade to the latest version · if centrally managed, roll out the updated Helm chart via Argo CD, Flux, or CI/CD · confirm it returns to Healthy.

Trade-offs & challenges

What I chose, and what it cost.

Overview over density

I gave the overview more vertical space, even though it pushes the table down. Seeing what matters first beat a few extra rows above the fold.

Modal over full page

A full page spread the content too thin and hurt scanning. A modal fit the volume and kept users anchored on the Sensors page.

Hardest part — hierarchy. It took many iterations to land on issues → coverage → everything else. I cut a duplicate coverage summary once it stayed visible without scrolling, and kept the details panel in one view rather than tabs to aid discoverability.

If I had more time

Where I'd take it next.

Historical coverage trends

A date selector and trend line. If telemetry stops, the break is obvious — and easy to correlate with a deployment change.

Interactive health distribution

Click any segment of the health widget to filter the sensors table instantly. The overview becomes a control, not just a chart.

Key takeaways

How I think about design.

  • 01

    Guide people through complexity instead of exposing it.

  • 02

    Verify success — don't ask users to.

  • 03

    Build trust through transparency.

  • 04

    Every issue should explain itself and offer the next action.

  • 05

    Design for five-second decisions.

  • 06

    Reduce cognitive load by matching familiar mental models.
